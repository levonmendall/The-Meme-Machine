# Executable architecture

The harness is external infrastructure. It restores the exact reviewed assembly from S git blobs, with immutable source files, no overlays and no native policy edits. The assembly digest is `08d5a491975345c859941c01cbde6ee3ef8b56a85026209fd497c14daa047659`. Importing a module or reviewing this package cannot release input; the isolated member role must pass signed authorization before its source-release guard is enabled.

The future process tree is controller → network-isolated trial → sequential member → native shared spawn pool of exactly two decoder/archive workers, plus its resource tracker. The native service retains one archive flight, its 64-entry owner queue and eight urgent reserved entries. Thread admissions precede targets, including Thread subclasses. Every child must have initialized and terminated origin/admission receipts; the verifier rechecks raw per-thread affinities and cgroup bindings rather than trusting an empty error list. A controller resource timeline must cover admission through teardown without missing or changed constraints.

The measured executor itself must have exactly two allocated dedicated vCPU and exactly 8 GiB allocated RAM. Affinity/quota cannot confer allocation credit. A 3/4/8/16/64-vCPU allocation capped to two CPUs fails; a larger visible/possible inventory with offline CPUs also fails. Independently trusted signed allocation evidence is required, including host/boot/time binding, complete ancestor inventory, durability capability bytes and an exact PID/start-time/executable allowlist for necessary system processes. Hidden ancestors, incomplete process inventory, competing work, restrictive limits, changed affinities, swap, ballooning or ambiguous allocation stop admission. Visible usable RAM is recorded separately from allocated RAM.

Cgroup-v2 collection preserves exact controller availability, subtree enablement, domain type and PRESENT/ABSENT/UNREADABLE interface states, plus documented root-exception annotations. Admission and the pure resource verifier independently prove complete root-to-leaf visibility and top-down applicability from captured raw membership, mounts, namespaces and directory identities. Every applicable incoming limit is enforced even when that node disables further propagation. Missing resource interfaces pass only with causal non-applicability proof; forged completeness and unsupported root exceptions fail. Signed allocation remains a separate requirement.

Storage checks bind mount/device/total/free bytes and signed file/directory fsync, SQLite WAL/locking and physical durability capabilities. Nondurable filesystems fail. The total budget includes immutable tape, working state, retained evidence, every simultaneous publication copy and 12 GiB headroom. Before preparation and every trial/member, declared work/publication reserves must fit current free space; continuous checks retain headroom. Native DB/WAL size remains strictly below 2 GiB. Inventories record every preserved byte. Publication never removes a sole local copy.

The semantic clock is frozen at wall 1800000000 and monotonic 100 during setup. One eight-byte shared anchor activates at first source-data release. All native processes then observe real elapsed time from that anchor; stdlib scheduling, OS waits and paired timing retain real clocks. Cadence catches up to original due times after a stall, without compensating pauses or rebasing ages/deadlines. The tape's economic event fields remain unchanged. Subscription acknowledgements do not activate the clock or release source data.

B admission and retrospective verification share `campaign.verify_capacity_prerequisite()`. It checks the actual A declaration bytes/hash, verifies A's complete sealed campaign with A's signed owner permit and allocation, rechecks capability bytes and declaration-bound ledger, and reads back every trial copy and final campaign copy. A STOPPED, INVALID, incomplete or unsuccessfully preserved campaign cannot satisfy B even when a raw A trial appears valid. Exact S/T, assembly, workload, tape, environment, executor, host and boot bindings are compared afterward.

B orders baseline→observed, observed→baseline, baseline→observed. Start precedes process startup and complete-cohort setup; end follows all native work, qualification reads/assessment, helper joins and measured persistence. Verification/publication occur afterward. Every pair must be complete, valid and identically bound. Baselines are positive integers, observed time cannot be below baseline, and `sum(observed−baseline) * 100 < sum(baseline)`. No subtraction, averaging of percentages, double counting, warmup, retry or replacement is accepted. The separate legacy short-reader wall fraction remains ≤1%.

C preserves exact original failed diagnostic predicates. A performance failure remains FAILED_DIAGNOSTIC and ends the campaign; later members are not replacements. Native safety is a separate result: threshold-triggered native EvidenceUnavailable ancestry and refusal telemetry, drained admitted work, bounded resources, integrity, protected evidence/receipts, committed ledger/episode/record/floor continuity and stale-authority refusal after native restart are mandatory. The witness copies and fsyncs actual state before reopening that same DB; it releases zero further frames, invokes no archive/retirement cycle and grants no attempted-work credit. After the genuine frozen MaintenanceRuntime creates the new generation in memory, the witness obtains runtime.generation and calls the existing native state.fence.health publisher for the current storage_maintenance metrics. Its native writer transaction durably commits exactly that generation while phase remains WARMING, before opening the refusal reader. No direct SQL publisher or generation relaxation is used. It pins one native reader transaction through the WARMING refusals and a self-contained raw SQLite backup. Each refusal has its own immutable receipt binding scope, exact reason, exact predicate observation time, phase, generation, native result and snapshot SHA-256; the refusal record binds the receipt SHA-256. Raw verification recomputes each refusal using that preserved state and its own timestamp with the frozen native reader/predicate. Final post-close OFF state, generation, integrity and protected/work/gap/floor continuity are verified independently from final `d/db`. A WARMING refusal can be `evidence_discontinuous` or `evidence_finalized_stale` while final OFF is `evidence_service_unavailable`. Wrapper-only termination and missing proof fail mandatory safety.

`DIAGNOSTIC_OVERLOAD_WITH_NATIVE_FAIL_CLOSED_PROOF` uses a separate terminal cohort record and a consumed, terminal `DIAGNOSTIC_OVERLOAD` ledger event. The member returns this protocol result only when native safety predicates pass; the trial immediately stops further members. The controller then independently verifies original diagnostic/failure ancestry, raw before/after native state and refusal health, exact consumed input, origins/lifetimes, resources and preservation. FAILED_DIAGNOSTIC remains preserved and capacity/observer credit stays false. Full C performance success and all A/B successes still require their complete cohorts.

Resource evidence receives paired real-monotonic/performance-clock boundaries, pre-spawn startup, actual first/last source release, confirmed helper termination and the measured endpoint. Monitoring stays active through measured persistence. The declared sampling policy has a 250 ms cadence, maximum 2 s sample gap, maximum 2 s admission/endpoint sampling tolerance and maximum 2 s uncertainty at process/thread lifetime edges. Each clock-pair read is limited to 10 ms, with at most 20 ms interval difference between clocks. Admission must precede startup and teardown must follow helper termination. Every required PID/start-time and TID is checked in all interior samples; admission/termination receipts cover short-lived helpers. Missing intervals, unrelated/truncated timelines, PID reuse and missing live processes/threads fail verification. These limits are explicit executable declaration fields and receive no timing subtraction.

Every started trial is consumed before its first child. On any failure the controller terminates the process group and joins tracked PID/start-time identities, preserves raw failure state, seals it and prevents advancement. Each trial receives a redundant readback copy. The final campaign copy includes declaration bytes, public signatures and capability evidence, the terminal ledger, raw trials and inventories. These copies are outside B timing and must all fit predeclared storage. Original evidence remains permanently retained.

Termination also checks reparented process-group helpers. Only a confirmed terminated tree permits removal of known `mN/d/db.sock` socket artifacts, with a durable receipt recording inode/device identity; every regular file remains intact. A strict sealing exception still records INVALID and attempts failure publication. The failure-only copier preserves every regular file and records unsupported special artifacts retained locally, with acceptance credit false. Final campaign sealing/publication failures record a stop, preserve failure bytes and prevent campaign acceptance. Successful trial-copy receipts are sealed with the campaign; the final copy's external `campaign.PRESERVATION.json` receipt binds declaration, ledger and campaign inventory hashes, and verification independently checks both retained and published copies.
