# METEORA

## 1. Market segment

Current SOL-paired census:

| TVL | Pools | Active 24h | Active share | 24h volume | 24h fees | Aggregate fee / TVL |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| < $10k | 94,839 | 1,047 | 1.10% | $14.56m | $377.6k | 10.87% |
| $10–25k | 169 | 129 | 76.33% | $16.08m | $249.8k | 9.07% |
| $25–50k | 89 | 72 | 80.90% | $9.15m | $171.6k | 5.78% |
| >= $50k | 170 | 153 | 90.00% | $212.94m | $865.4k | 1.49% |

The < $10k universe is overwhelmingly inactive. High aggregate fee density there is generated by a very small active tail, so **small TVL is not an entry signal**.

The strongest repeatable current segment is **$25k–$50k TVL**:
- ~81% have current 24h volume;
- median volume/TVL ~0.87x/day;
- median fee/TVL ~0.53%/day;
- p75 ~4.42%, p90 ~11.82%, p95 ~39.74%.

The $10k–$25k segment is attractive but more skewed: high aggregate fee density with weaker typical fee density. It should be treated as a higher-risk secondary hunting ground.

>= $50k pools are the most consistently active and deepest, but fee density is lower. They remain useful when fee density itself becomes exceptional.

### Conclusion

**TVL is a capacity/risk variable, not the primary alpha variable.**  
The primary opportunity variable is **fee production relative to usable liquidity**.

## 2. Pool age

The final normalized census shows most pools in every TVL segment are >=7 days old. Young pools are rare, but the deterministic active-pool sample shows much higher fee density in young pools:

- $25k–$50k sampled active pools:
  - 1–6h: median fee/TVL ~86.4%/day (n=2)
  - 6–24h: ~21.3% (n=2)
  - 1–7d: ~7.50% (n=8)
  - >=7d: ~0.83% (n=41)

The sample sizes in the youngest buckets are too small to turn age into a hard gate.

### Conclusion

**Pool age is a ranking boost, not a qualification rule.**
Priority order, all else equal:
1. 1–24h
2. 1–7d
3. mature pools with exceptional current fee density

Do not enter merely because a pool/token is new. The new-pool effect is valuable only when accompanied by real liquidity, two-way volume, and executable exit depth.

### Conclusion

Use a **live, pre-entry fee-to-usable-liquidity metric as the primary ranker**.

A research threshold corresponding to the historical API regime `feePerTvl24h >= 5` is the best-supported starting qualification region. A >=20 regime is exceptionally strong but should be treated as a higher-volatility/high-selectivity tier rather than the universal target.

## 4. Holding time

Conditional on the historical high-fee regime (`feePerTvl24h >=5`):

| Hold | positions | win rate | deployed return |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| <15m | 60,595 | 93.9% | 0.99% |
| 15–60m | 24,166 | 94.9% | 1.78% |
| 1–4h | 21,307 | 92.6% | 2.85% |
| 4–24h | 14,803 | 92.3% | **5.17%** |
| >=24h | 1,681 | 93.0% | 11.22% |

The >=24h group has high returns but materially less breadth and substantially greater regime/inventory exposure.

### Conclusion

The old ultra-short horizon is rejected by the market evidence.

**Core holding regime: 4–24 hours, with event-driven early exit.**  
Positions may remain >24h only while fee density, two-way flow, range occupancy, and unwindability remain favorable.

The exit clock should be a maximum-risk boundary, not the source of the trade.

The independent profitable-wallet study separately found 23/24 profitable wallets dominated by >50-bin ranges.

### Conclusion

Width is mainly a **risk / repeatability / capital-efficiency tradeoff**, not the primary alpha source.

**Default robust width: 51–100 bins.**
- Narrow toward 26–50 when fee density is extreme and recent displacement is contained.
- Widen beyond 100 when volatility requires it and the expected fee density remains sufficient after dilution.
- Normalize by bin step / price distance rather than blindly applying one bin count to every pool.

## 7. Final Meteora strategy

### Discovery
Rank active SOL-paired DLMM pools primarily by:
1. live pre-entry fee / usable local liquidity;
2. volume / usable local liquidity;
3. persistence of that activity;
4. two-way flow / reversion quality;
5. local executable depth and stress-unwind cost;
6. pool age as a secondary boost.

TVL preference:
- **Primary hunting band: $25k–$50k**
- Secondary: $10k–$25k with stronger depth/risk controls
- >=$50k when fee density is exceptional
- Avoid the broad <$10k universe; consider only its rare active tail.

### Entry
Enter only when:
- fee density is in the historically positive regime;
- active/local liquidity can absorb the paper position and exit;
- flow is not persistently one-way;
- the proposed range is being touched or is immediately relevant;
- expected fee capture remains positive after actual entry/exit/rebalance/unwind costs.

Do not use absolute volume, TVL, or age alone.

### Range
- Default broad range: **51–100 bins**, normalized to price distance.
- 26–50 bins only in contained-volatility, very-high-fee regimes.
- Wider than 100 when volatility/range-travel requires it.
- Default to near-active/two-sided or evidence-supported asymmetric placement; do not blindly park a one-sided range away from current flow.

### Size
Size from **local executable depth and stress-unwind capacity**, never from total pool TVL alone.

### Management
- No timer-based rebalance.
- Hold while fee density remains elevated and flow remains economically healthy.
- Rebalance only when the active price is leaving the productive portion of the range **and** the expected remaining fee edge pays for the rebalance.
- Prefer exit/re-entry to churn when the regime has changed.

### Exit
Exit early on:
- sustained one-way drift;
- fee-density collapse;
- loss of two-way flow;
- active price leaving the economically productive range;
- unwind/slippage deterioration;
- local liquidity collapse.

Core expected hold: **4–24h**, not 60 seconds.

### Confidence
**High for the strategy shape; medium for exact numeric fee-density thresholds until the historical API field is reproduced point-in-time prospectively.**

# RAMSES

## 1. Market structure

The Ramses evidence does not support copying the Meteora strategy.

Ramses DLMM is a very small-liquidity, very-high-turnover venue. Current public venue data shows roughly:
- ~$400k TVL,
- ~$42.8m 30-day volume,
- ~$295k 30-day fees.

That is roughly 107x monthly volume/TVL. Activity is highly concentrated and time-varying.

The natural 20-minute factory study scanned all 277 authenticated factory pools but observed only one unique recently active pool. That pool had one-way flow, no meaningful reversion/chop, and did not supply a natural fee-harvesting lifecycle.

Current market listings also show leading pairs with hundreds of thousands of dollars of daily volume against only hundreds to low thousands of dollars of displayed +/-2% depth.

## 2. What this means economically

Ramses is primarily a **burst-liquidity / adverse-selection market**.

The profitable segment is not defined well by:
- TVL bucket,
- pool age,
- or token age.

It is defined by whether a pool is temporarily producing very high fees against local liquidity **without** converting the LP into the losing side of a directional move.

Because the venue is fee-only, emissions should not be counted as strategy edge.

## 3. Final Ramses strategy

### Universe
Prioritize pools where the assets have an external fair-value reference:
- stable / stable,
- major crypto pairs,
- tokenized/RWA assets with observable off-chain or cross-venue reference pricing.

Permissionless/new assets are not excluded, but they receive no age premium by themselves.

### Trigger
Do **not** stay permanently deployed.

Wait for a live fee pulse:
1. multiple recent swaps, not a single print;
2. high fees / active-bin liquidity;
3. high volume / active-bin liquidity;
4. both trade directions represented;
5. gross bin travel materially exceeds net directional displacement;
6. pool price remains reasonably anchored to independent fair value;
7. executable exit depth is known;
8. projected fees exceed actual gas + unwind + inventory-risk reserve.

One-way volume is a rejection, not an opportunity.

### Range
Use an **adaptive centered / fair-value-anchored range**, not a fixed bin count:
- range should cover the recent short-horizon realized bin excursion with a modest buffer;
- narrow when the pulse is stationary/choppy;
- widen only when the incremental expected fees compensate for diluted capital efficiency.

There is not enough historical Ramses operator evidence to justify an exact universal number of bins.

### Size
Ramses capacity should be based on **executable local depth**, not factory-pool TVL.
Current displayed depth implies that many pools support only small positions without becoming their own exit problem.

### Hold / management
Use **pulse-duration holds**, generally minutes rather than hours:
- remain while flow is two-way and fee generation stays elevated;
- do not rebalance repeatedly through a directional move;
- if the pulse ends, exit;
- if price breaks from fair value or flow becomes one-way, exit immediately;
- re-enter later only after a new independent pulse forms.

No fixed 30-minute or 60-minute policy is justified by the present evidence.

### Confidence
**Moderate for the strategy architecture; low-to-medium for exact thresholds and hold duration.**
Ramses lacks the deep historical LP-position panel available for Meteora. A precise fixed-width/fixed-time rule would be false precision.

# Final answers to the original market questions

| Question | Meteora | Ramses |
|---|---|---|
| New coins/pools? | Useful boost when activity is real; not enough alone. 1–7d appears higher fee-density, but sparse. | No demonstrated age edge. |
| Small TVL? | Not broadly. <$10k is 98.9% inactive. | TVL is a poor primary variable. |
| Best TVL region? | $25k–$50k is the strongest repeatable current hunting band; $10k–$25k is higher-skew secondary. | No stable TVL band established. |
| Fees? | **Primary signal.** Historical fee-density regime is the strongest discriminator. | **Primary signal**, but must be paired with two-way flow and fair-value anchoring. |
| Volume? | Use volume relative to local usable liquidity, not raw volume. | Same, even more important. |
| Bin depth? | Size from local depth/unwind; broad 51–100-bin range is most repeatable. | Local depth is the main capacity constraint; adaptive narrow/medium range. |
| Flow? | Two-way/reverting flow strongly preferred. | Essential; one-way flow is adverse selection. |
| Time? | **4–24h core regime**, early event-driven exit; longer only while regime persists. | Pulse-duration, typically short; no fixed hold justified. |
| Rebalancing? | Event-driven, sparing. | Prefer exit/re-entry over chasing a directional move. |

## Bottom line

**Meteora's best-supported strategy is a high-fee-density, broad-range, medium-duration liquidity strategy—not a newborn-token snipe and not a 60-second narrow-range trade.**

The market evidence says to find active pools where fees are unusually high relative to usable liquidity, with the current $25k–$50k segment offering the best balance of repeatability and density, deploy a broad 51–100-bin range adjusted for bin step/volatility, and hold roughly 4–24 hours while two-way flow and fee density persist.

**Ramses' best-supported strategy is fundamentally different: episodic, short-duration fee-pulse liquidity around externally priceable assets, sized against tiny local exit depth, entered only after genuine two-way activity appears, and exited immediately when the pulse turns directional or decays.**

The analysis does not support one universal DLMM strategy across the two venues.
